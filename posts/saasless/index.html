SaasLess

architecture fastapi nosaas philosophy

There is a word missing from the industry’s vocabulary. We have SaaS. We have self-hosted. We have open-source. But we do not have a word for what happens when someone deploys an open-source pipeline on free infrastructure and lets anyone use it without charging, without accounts, without dashboards, without pricing pages.

I am calling it SaaSless.

The Architecture

note2cms now runs on FastAPI Cloud – the platform built by Sebastian Ramirez, the creator of FastAPI itself. The framework author’s own infrastructure, serving the framework’s tallest testament.

The database is one Neon table. One row per user. Six columns: token, repo, branch, site URL, title, theme. That is the entire schema. The database wakes, executes one SELECT, and goes back to sleep. The compute cost of a publish is measured in microseconds of database time. Everything else is GitHub’s problem.

The content lives in the user’s own GitHub repo. Not on my server. Not in my database. In their repo, under their git history, owned by them, exportable with one git clone. The taxonomy is a JSON file in the repo. The source Markdown is in the repo. The rendered HTML is in the repo. I hold nothing but a row in a phonebook.

The Registration

You visit a form. The first thing you see is a theme dropdown. Pick one. The entire form transforms – fonts, colors, borders, personality. You are previewing your blog before you type a single character.

Fill in your GitHub repo. Paste your fine-grained PAT. Choose your theme. Hit register. Get a token back. Start publishing.

No email. No password. No verification. No onboarding flow. No welcome email. No tutorial. No dashboard you will never visit. One token. One endpoint. That is the relationship.

The Economics

FastAPI Cloud free tier. Neon free tier. GitHub Pages free tier. Total infrastructure cost: zero dollars.

The pipeline wakes when someone publishes. It parses Markdown, renders HTML through Jinja2, fetches the taxonomy from the user’s repo, updates it, pushes a commit with four files, returns a permalink, and dies. The server is idle 99.99 percent of the time. The database is asleep 99.99 percent of the time. The static HTML is served by GitHub’s CDN at the speed of light.

A domain name costs twelve dollars a year. That is the total cost of SaaSless publishing.

The Credits

I did not invent anything.

Sebastian Ramirez wrote FastAPI. Armin Ronacher wrote Jinja2. Tom Christie wrote httpx. Samuel Colvin wrote Pydantic. Charlie Marsh wrote uv. Marcelo Trylesinski maintains Uvicorn. Each released their work under permissive licenses with conviction.

I gathered seven libraries behind four endpoints and pointed them at a specific problem. The composition is the contribution. The components are replaceable. The protocol persists.

And Armin – the creator of Flask, the framework FastAPI replaced – is an investor and advisor to FastAPI Cloud. The platform his tool’s successor built. No spite. No territorial defense. Just infrastructure lifting infrastructure. The quiet aristocracy of people who build small things that work together to create something big.

The Difference

Every SaaS publishing platform stores your content on their servers, runs their compute on every page view, charges monthly for the privilege, and holds your words hostage behind an export button.

note2cms stores nothing. Computes nothing at read time. Charges nothing. Holds nothing. Your content is in your repo. Your blog is static HTML on a CDN. The pipeline exists for the three seconds between your POST and your permalink. Then it dies.

SaaSless is not a business model. It is the absence of one. It is the proof that the business model was never necessary – that the infrastructure the SaaS charges for can be eliminated by correct architecture.

The form is live. The themes preview in real-time. The pipeline publishes in under a second. The database has one table. The server has four endpoints. The license is MIT, issued with conviction.

We cannot lose what we do not hold.

Deployed on FastAPI Cloud. Backed by Neon. Rendered by Jinja2. Served by GitHub. Built by everyone. Owned by no one.
